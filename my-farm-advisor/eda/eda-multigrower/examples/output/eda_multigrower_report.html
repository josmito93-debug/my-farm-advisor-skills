Field boundaries

1. Field size distribution

Areas span three orders of magnitude and the distributions overlap heavily, so the cross-grower Kruskal-Wallis result is not significant (p=0.88). Medians differ (IL 59 ac, IA 150 ac, NE 74 ac) but a 3.2-acre IL meadow polygon and a 612-acre IL field drive the tails. Compare acre-weighted totals, not field counts.

2. Field shape vs size

Compactness rises with area: small parcels inherit road-frontage geometry, larger parcels approach section-line rectangles (shape index near 1.13). Kruskal-Wallis on compactness: H=1.56, p=0.46, so shape differences are descriptive rather than statistically separable at n=10.

3. Seasonal growing degree days

Seasonal GDD (base 10 degC, Apr-Sep) separates growers clearly (H=125.4, p=5.9e-28). NE averages 2030 GDD (range 1919-2133), the highest of the three sites in every year, while field-to-field spread stays modest.

4. Growing-season precipitation

Seasonal precipitation also separates growers (H=33.6, p=5.0e-08). NE is the driest site on average (444 mm versus 564 mm at IA), and 2024 is visibly the wettest year in all three states.

5. CDL crop composition

Corn is the largest single crop everywhere, but the split is uneven: NE 73% corn / 27% soybean versus IL 52% / 42% and IA 53% / 36%. 'Other' is a small residual, so the comparison is effectively corn-soybean rotation intensity.

6. Field-level crop sequence

The heatmap exposes within-field rotation behavior: most rows alternate corn and soybean, while a few fields stay in corn for consecutive years or hold pasture. This is the within-field level of the analysis.

Levels of comparison

Within a field: crop sequence by year (figure 6) and year-to-year weather at the same centroid (figures 3-4). Across fields within a grower: area and shape spread (figures 1-2), composition spread (figure 5). Across growers: IL vs IA vs NE tests above and the three-panel map.

Limitations

OSM field polygons are volunteer-mapped, so field counts and shapes are a sample, not a complete farm inventory. CDL is 30 m and reports pixel composition rather than management. Weather comes from a gridded reanalysis product, not on-farm sensors. CDL covers 2019-2023 while weather covers 2021-2025, so cross-category tests use the 2021-2023 overlap. Soil properties are not analysed here.
